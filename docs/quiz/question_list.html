4. Vertex coloring: limits of approximation (2)

Example 1 · aa-g-vci-interval · level facile · notes
Interval graph with vertices $1,\dots,6$ and closed intervals $I_{1}=[1,5]$, $I_{2}=[5,8]$, $I_{3}=[6,9]$, $I_{4}=[7,11]$, $I_{5}=[8,9]$, $I_{6}=[8,9]$ (two vertices are adjacent if their intervals intersect). How many colors does the greedy coloring use with the vertices in order of non-decreasing left endpoint?
  1. $3$
  2. $4$
  3. $5$
  4. $6$
Explanation. On an interval graph that order gives $\omega(G)$ colors, the largest number of intervals with a common point: here $5$, and it is optimal since $\chi(G)\ge\omega(G)$. The bound $\Delta(G)+1=6$ holds for every order but is weaker.

5. Maximum clique: limits of approximation (2)

Example 1 · aa-g-cli-product · level facile · notes
Two graphs on the vertices $\{1,2,3,4\}$: $G$ with $E=\{\{2,4\}\}$ and $H$ with $F=\{\{2,3\},\{3,4\}\}$. How many vertices does the strong product $G\boxtimes H$ have, and what is its clique number?
  1. $16$ vertices and $\omega=5$
  2. $16$ vertices and $\omega=2$
  3. $16$ vertices and $\omega=4$
  4. $8$ vertices and $\omega=4$
Explanation. $G\boxtimes H$ has $4\cdot4=16$ vertices and $\omega(G\boxtimes H)=\omega(G)\,\omega(H)=2\cdot2=4$: the product of two cliques is a clique, and every clique of the product lies in such a product.

How long is a minimum spanning tree $T$, whose length $d(T)$ is a lower bound on the optimal tour?
  1. $15$
  2. $17$
  3. $32$
  4. $16$
Explanation. Prim's (or Kruskal's) algorithm gives $d(T)=16$. Removing an edge from the optimal tour gives a spanning tree, so $d(T)\le z(I)$; the double tree gives a tour of length at most $2d(T)=32$.
